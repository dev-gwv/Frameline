How the product works

Two people use Frameline. The photographer runs events from the admin app on a laptop. Guests open a link on their phone, take a selfie, and leave with their photos. Every decision below makes one of those two jobs shorter.

UX principles

Photos first, gold for action

The interface uses warm neutrals and a dark sidebar so pictures stand out. Gold marks the one action that matters on each screen and any selected photo.

Never make them refresh

Uploads, imports, face indexing and camera sync stream into the grid, and progress never blocks other work.

Decide once, with presets

Most events are one of a few kinds. Pick a preset when you create the event and adjust later only if needed.

Say what guests will see

Privacy and download settings sit next to a plain-language summary and a real guest preview.

A beginner can finish it

Every screen asks plain questions in order (where, what, how big) and shows the result on a real photo. Expert controls fold away under "More options", and every icon has a label or a tooltip.

Phone-first for guests

The guest gallery is a fast PWA, with no forced app install and download or buy always one tap away.

Key flows

Tech stack: Cloudflare-native, lowest running cost

TypeScript across the product, run almost entirely on Cloudflare. Photos live in R2, which charges nothing for downloads, the biggest cost in photo delivery. Metadata sits in D1, face vectors in Vectorize, and the heavy image and face work runs in Containers that only bill while they are working. The one exception is camera FTP, which needs a small VPS.

Monthly running cost, worked examples

Assumptions: each photo stores about 7 MB (original plus 3 sizes), processing takes about 1 CPU-second, and each photo has about 3 faces stored as 512-dimension vectors. Prices are Cloudflare list prices at the time of writing, so check current pricing before you budget.

Beta · 10 studios

≈ $10/mo

30k photos/mo, 300 GB stored, 20k visits. Workers $5 · R2 storage $4.35 · Containers $0.15 · Vectorize $0.05 · downloads $0.

Launch · 100 studios

≈ $85/mo

400k photos/mo, 3 TB stored, 2 TB downloaded. R2 storage $45 · R2 writes $4.50 · Containers $9.30 · Vectorize $16 · Workers $7.40. The same downloads on S3 would add about $180.

Scale · 1,000 studios

≈ $880/mo

4M photos/mo, 30 TB stored. R2 storage $450 · R2 requests $95 · Containers $100 · Vectorize $165 · Workers $57 · D1 $10. Revenue at this scale is about $8.4k/mo.

The one cost to manage: storage

Move originals from expired events to R2 Infrequent Access (about $0.01 instead of $0.015 per GB) and delete them after the grace period. This cuts the largest line on the bill by 40–60%. Face data for expired events is deleted on the same schedule, which is also a privacy promise to guests.

Trade-offs we accept

D1 is SQLite

There is one writer per database and a 10 GB limit each. Photo rows are about 400 bytes, so tens of millions fit before we split by studio. Postgres on Neon is the fallback, at about $19–69/mo.

No face model in Workers AI

Face detection and embeddings run in InsightFace inside a Container. That costs about $0.00002 per photo, compared with about $1 per 1,000 photos on AWS Rekognition.

FTP can't run on Cloudflare

Camera sync needs a raw TCP server. A €4 VPS runs SFTPGo, writes to R2 and calls the API. Everything else stays on Cloudflare.

Harder to switch vendors

Keeping business logic in plain TypeScript, and D1 access behind Drizzle, makes a move to Postgres and S3-compatible storage a matter of weeks, not a rewrite.

Design system: Gilt

Gilt draws on the gallery frame and the gold-leaf album cover. The palette is warm ivory and obsidian, with gold only where the photographer acts or selects. A high-contrast serif gives headings an editorial feel, a clean grotesque keeps the interface easy to read, and a mono face handles IDs, frame numbers and money.
